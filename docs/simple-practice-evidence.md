# Evidence behind the two practice paths

Checked 22 September 2026. These are product defaults informed by learning experiments, not a Quran protocol with a validated optimal repetition count.

## Memorize: read, recall, return

The recommended sequence is one ayah at a time: two complete visible recitations, hide the text for one independent attempt, continue through the selected ayahs, then return for a second hidden attempt at each ayah. Finish by connecting the selected range without upcoming text. For a single ayah, the second immediate attempt provides much less spacing; returning tomorrow is especially important. Long ayahs may need teacher-guided phrase practice before a whole-ayah attempt. This sequence and its counts are implementation choices, not findings established by the studies below.

- **Include hidden recall.** In Roediger and Karpicke's two prose experiments, extra study helped immediate performance, while retrieval helped delayed retention. Their tests scored remembered ideas, not faithful word-for-word recitation. This supports moving beyond visible rereading; it does not quantify a Quran benefit. [Original study, 2006](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2006/2006_Roediger_Karpicke_PsychSci.pdf).
- **Return after other work and on later days.** Rawson and Dunlosky tested 533 students learning conceptual material with restudy and retrieval. Their recommended balance was three initial correct recalls and three later relearning sessions. That finding supports repeated successful recall across occasions; it does not establish two visible plus two hidden recitations, or any fixed Quran count. The connected attempt can supply another retrieval opportunity, but it should not be described as proof of lasting mastery. [Original study abstract, 2011](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21707204/).
- **Keep the schedule simple.** Two experiments using educational texts found benefits from repeated retrieval and feedback but no long-term advantage of expanding over equally spaced retrieval under their tested conditions. A next-day review followed by later reviews is a practical default; a complex interval configuration is unnecessary for this first flow. [Original study, 2010](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2010/2010_Karpicke_Roediger_MC.pdf).
- **Repair errors, then try again.** In vocabulary learning, supplying the correct response after an error improved later retention. The experiment does not establish an optimal audio-cue delay for Quran recitation. A short, calm correction followed by another attempt is a reasonable application. [Authors' report of the original experiment, 2005](https://www.yorku.ca/ncepeda/publications/PCWR2005.html).

Count visible study and hidden recall separately. A text reveal or corrective recording makes the current recall attempt assisted; it should lead to a fresh hidden attempt rather than award independent-recall credit. Show already-recited words if useful, but keep the upcoming answer hidden. These are measurement choices that preserve the distinction between reading and retrieval. Do not automatically escalate repetitions merely because the microphone failed to recognize speech.

## Honest, quiet feedback

Use a small completion tick for a full matched pass, a brief retry cue for convincing mismatch evidence, and neutral wording when audio cannot be assessed. In the current recognizer, `matched_indices` includes both `ok` and `unsure` word verdicts; coverage is therefore insufficient to certify every pronunciation or tajwid detail. Automated passage matching must not be labeled a comprehensive recitation grade. A count should require the entire intended passage, fresh speech, and no unresolved issue or assistance. [Primary implementation: emission.ts](../packages/core/src/recitation/emission.ts), [correction.ts](../packages/core/src/recitation/correction.ts).

Revision can keep live tracking while offering the expected ayah recording on a confirmed difficulty. Repeated evidence from a **new attempt**, rather than repeated callbacks for the same utterance, can reveal enlarged text with a small red label. Neither enlargement nor a specific retry threshold is a proven memory treatment; those are usability decisions. A quiet inline cue is sufficient without an alarming modal.

## Recorded correction audio

EveryAyah exposes Alafasy verse recordings using three-digit surah plus three-digit ayah filenames. The directory includes `001001.mp3`, `002001.mp3`, and `112002.mp3`, plus separate opening files. For example, the expected 112:2 cue is `https://everyayah.com/data/Alafasy_128kbps/112002.mp3`. A HEAD request on 22 September 2026 returned HTTP 200, `audio/mpeg`, byte ranges, and `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`. This verifies current availability, not a service guarantee. Preserve surah/ayah mapping and existing Bismillah handling; do not use `000` entries as numbered ayahs. [Provider directory](https://everyayah.com/data/Alafasy_128kbps/), [verified 112:2 recording](https://everyayah.com/data/Alafasy_128kbps/112002.mp3), [current text-offset implementation](../web/frontend/src/hifz/lesson.ts).

Use a reusable `HTMLAudioElement`. Set `crossOrigin = 'anonymous'` **before** assigning the URL: this app requires cross-origin isolation, and the verified response allows CORS. Await `play()`, which can reject when playback is disallowed; offer a small Listen action if needed. Release playback and resume listening on `ended`, error, cancellation, and navigation. [HTML media specification](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/media.html#dom-media-play), [CORS settings specification](https://html.spec.whatwg.org/multipage/urls-and-fetching.html#cors-settings-attributes), [app headers](../web/frontend/vite.config.ts).

Implementation recommendation: play the **expected** ayah in full rather than synthesize Quran speech or guess word-level cut points. Gate recognizer input and automatic completion during playback and a short speaker-tail window, so the app cannot count its own recording. Resume automatically without a new microphone prompt. Audio failure must leave practice usable and must not mark the learner wrong. Streaming these cues needs internet even though recognition runs locally. Retain provider/reciter attribution in supporting information and do not imply the repository's code license licenses external recordings. Text, numbering, reading, and recordings should remain a consistent set; pronunciation and stopping conventions still benefit from qualified human checking.
